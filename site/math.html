Math Behind Tech

The mathematics under the software you use every day, worked out with numbers small enough to check on paper. RSA with p = 3 and q = 11 before RSA with 617-digit primes. Zip on ABRACADABRA before zip on a Docker image.

Most explanations of the maths in technology stop at the idea. "RSA relies on the difficulty of factoring large numbers." True, and useless: you close the tab knowing exactly as much as before. These chapters go the other way. The theory is kept to a screen, and then every single number is computed in front of you, by hand, small enough that you can redo it with a pencil and catch us if we slip.

How every chapter is built

Each chapter follows the same shape, so once you have read one you know where to look in the next.

01 · Where you meet it

The four or five places in real systems where this maths is doing the work right now: a padlock in a browser, a file in your home directory, a header on an API call.

02 · The idea in one breath

The theory in plain words, one screen long. Every term defined the first time it appears. No prerequisites assumed beyond arithmetic.

03 · The tools

Every mathematical operation the chapter needs, introduced one at a time with its own one-line example. If you can follow the tools you can follow the chapter.

04 · Worked examples

Every example sits on its own cream sheet, visually apart from the theory: a one-line problem, the working as rows you can check one at a time, and the answer.

05 · Run it

The same computation in standard-library Python, then through the production tool, so the toy and the real thing line up field by field.

06 · Toy vs production

A table of what changes at real scale and why, followed by a few exercises with hidden answers, and the primary sources the chapter rests on.
