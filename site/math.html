Math Behind Tech

The maths under the software you use every day, worked out with numbers small enough to check on paper. Not proofs to admire. Things you can redo with a pencil, then run for real.

HTTPS · Chapter 01

You opened this page over HTTPS. The padlock is two prime numbers multiplied together. With p = 3 and q = 11 you can build the key, lock a message, unlock it, and then break it, all with a pencil.

GZIP · Chapter 02

This page reached you compressed. Most pages are three to five times smaller on the wire than on your screen.

Two tricks do it: spend fewer bits on common letters, and point backwards at text you already sent. The chapter ends by reading a real gzip file bit by bit.

Something you ran today minted a UUID and looked up a key in a hash map. Both rest on one party trick: put 23 people in a room and the odds that two share a birthday are better than even.
